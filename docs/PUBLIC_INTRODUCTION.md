# Beis

**Collaborative research and publishing for Torah scholarship.**

---

Beis is GitHub for Torah research. Teams of students work together on a shared project — picking up sections, drafting, reviewing each other's work, getting it approved — and the result ships as a published sefer, digital or print. The platform handles coordination, peer review, attribution, AI-assisted source work, and the production pipeline.

The subjects are the full range of Torah scholarship: Chassidus, Kabbalah, Bavli, Yerushalmi, Rishonim, Acharonim, Zohar, teshuvos. Both tracks share the same platform, the same peer review, and the same publication pipeline.

---

## The problem it solves

Most serious Torah scholarship projects that should exist don't — a comprehensive treatment of a sugya across the full corpus, a definitive translation, a Kabbalah index, an organized commentary on a major Chassidic work. The coordination cost is too high, the funding model too fragile, the production overhead beyond what a chaburah can absorb.

An individual chaver also loses most of his research seder to rote work: cross-referencing, source-pulling, bibliography, formatting. The deep work — sevara, chiddush, iyun — is a fraction of the time spent.

---

## How Beis works

A rosh chaburah opens a project and defines the scope. Chaverim claim sections, draft, and submit. AI agents pull sources, surface parallels, and handle production tasks. Peer reviewers sharpen the work. The mashpia approves. The finished work flows into the publication pipeline — digital or print-ready.

Every contribution is attributed and searchable. Accepted work accumulates to each chaver's record. Donors choose specific projects and see exactly where their support lands. Major funding decisions are made by contributors, weighted by their contribution record.

---

## The role of AI

AI agents function as junior research assistants: pulling sources, flagging parallels, drafting bibliographies, formatting footnotes, preparing manuscripts. They are assigned roles by the rosh chaburah and their contributions are attributed like any other member's. They don't pasken, don't write chiddushim, and don't substitute for the human work. They eliminate the rote work so the chaver can do what only he can do.

---

## What gets produced

The deliverable is concrete: a published sefer, a finished encyclopedia volume, a translated tractate, an indexed corpus — in formats that didn't require a full publishing operation before. This is Torah production that is distributed, AI-leveraged, transparently funded, and collectively credited.
